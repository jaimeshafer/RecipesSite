date: 2026-08-08

## Bird

- ~5 lb whole chicken
- Spatchcocked

## Seasoning

- Meat Church Honey Hog base
- Light Holy Gospel over top

## Primary Cook

- Started prep/seasoning ~2:30 PM.
- On smoker 3:15 PM.
- Smoker: 350°F.
- At ~1 hour: breast measured ~127-133°F.
- Around 4:45 PM: most areas were >165°F; one breast area was ~157°F.
- Pulled at 5:00 PM.
- Total cook: ~1 hour 45 minutes.
- Rested ~10 minutes.

## Primary Cook Results

- Chicken remained juicy.
- Wings and legs had crispier skin.
- Breast skin was still somewhat rubbery.
- 350°F straight through did **not** produce dramatically better skin than the previous low-smoke/high-heat method.
- The 350°F method was faster, but not necessarily better.

## Secondary Smoke Experiment

- Pulled/chunked part of the chicken.
- Saved regular pulled chicken as a control.
- Put experimental chicken into a small foil pan.
- Added a small amount of butter.
- Left pan **uncovered**.
- Reduced smoker to 165°F.
- Gave chicken approximately 45 minutes total additional smoke exposure.
- Stirred/exposed chicken during the secondary smoke.

## Result

- **Success.**
- Second-smoked pulled chicken tasted noticeably better.
- More smoke flavor than the control chicken.
- Stayed juicy despite the additional ~45 minutes on the smoker.
- Secondary smoking was worthwhile.

## Next Cook

- Keep the secondary pulled-chicken smoke: **165°F / uncovered / ~45 minutes**.
- Primary cook still needs improvement for breast skin.
- Consider testing: 225°F for ~30-40 minutes for initial smoke → 400°F finish for better skin.

## Current Takeaway

Secondary smoking **after** pulling/chunking appears to be an effective way to get noticeably smokier flavor into pulled chicken without drying it out.
