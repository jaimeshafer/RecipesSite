This is not a collection of generic internet recipes. It documents how Jaime actually cooks: start with a working method, record the cook and feedback, make specific changes, then repeat until the result is reliable.

## Development Process

1. Start with a working recipe or idea.
2. Record the actual cook.
3. Record results and family feedback.
4. Identify one or two specific changes to test.
5. Repeat.
6. Promote the recipe once it is reliably repeatable.

Failed experiments stay in the cookbook. They explain why the preferred method exists and prevent repeating the same mistake.

## Recipe Status

- **Experimental** — First attempt or major variables are still being tested.
- **Draft** — Successful, but needs additional cooks.
- **Dialed-In** — Repeated successfully and considered the preferred method.

Dialed-In recipes can still receive version updates.

## What to Record

### Before the Cook

- Date
- Meat/product, weight, and source
- Trim, seasoning, seasoning time, and fuel/pellets

### During the Cook

- Start time and cooker temperature
- Temperature checkpoints
- Wrap timing and reason for wrapping
- Temperature changes, spritzing, or basting
- Finish time, temperature, and probe tenderness

### Rest, Results, and Next Steps

- Vent time, hold temperature/duration, and serving time
- Flavor, smoke, bark, moisture, tenderness, fat rendering, and appearance
- Family feedback
- What worked, what did not, and only one or two next variables to test

## Cooking Priorities

1. Flavor
2. Tenderness and texture
3. Repeatability
4. Smoke flavor
5. Bark
6. Presentation

The goal is practical backyard barbecue: meat-forward flavor, noticeable smoke, a strong bark, moderate sweetness, and sauce that is optional rather than necessary.

## Current Baselines

- **Brisket:** 225°F → bark set → butcher paper → 250°F → probe tender → vent → 150-160°F warm hold. The flat's probe tenderness determines doneness.
- **Ribs:** St. Louis cut with Texas Sugar + light Holy Gospel. The final method is still in development; use larger, meatier racks for future tests.
- **Chicken:** Honey Hog + light Holy Gospel, with low smoke followed by a high-temperature finish for better skin.
- **Wings:** 375°F for approximately 40 minutes; Texas Sugar or Holy Voodoo.
- **Sides:** Develop with the same experiment → feedback → refinement process so they support complete barbecue meals.
