## Current Preferred Direction

**225°F for 25 minutes → 375°F until rendered/crisp and ready.**

This is a successful direction, but not fully finalized. The next cook should test only a shorter 375°F finishing period.

## Cook History

### 9/27/26 — Smoked-Then-Hot Wings

**Status:** Successful experiment / current preferred direction

#### Amount

- Approximately 5 lb chicken wings.

#### Seasoning

- Batch split between Meat Church Texas Sugar and Meat Church Holy Voodoo.

#### Preparation

- Wings dried thoroughly, seasoned, and refrigerated uncovered before cooking.

#### Cook

- Traeger at 225°F.
- Wings on at 2:25 PM.
- Smoked at 225°F for 25 minutes.
- At 2:50 PM, increased smoker to 375°F.
- Flipped around 3:10 PM.
- Removed at 3:30 PM.
- Total cook time: approximately 65 minutes.
- Total high-heat portion: approximately 40 minutes.

#### Results

- Wings tasted good and the method was successful.
- Internal temperatures at removal were generally 190°F+, with one wing measuring approximately 207°F.
- The wings were allowed to cook somewhat longer than necessary.
- Retain the initial 225°F smoke stage for the next experiment because the overall result was good.

#### Next Experiment

- Keep the seasoning and 225°F × 25-minute smoke stage unchanged.
- Increase to 375°F as before.
- Begin checking/probing after approximately 25 minutes at 375°F rather than waiting 40 minutes.
- Target roughly 175-190°F on the larger pieces while also judging by rendered/crisp skin.
- Remove individual pieces as they become ready if necessary.
- Goal: retain the added smoke and good skin while avoiding unnecessary cooking past the desired temperature.

### Previous Straight-375°F Cook

#### Prep

- Pat wings very dry.
- Season approximately 30 minutes before cooking.
- Tested:
  - Meat Church Texas Sugar
  - Meat Church Holy Voodoo

#### Cook

- Smoker at 375°F.
- Cook approximately 40 minutes.
- Cook until skin is crisp and chicken is properly cooked.

#### Result

- "Really good."
- Excellent crispy skin.
- Much better skin than low-and-slow method.
- Wanted slightly more smoke flavor, but texture was considerably better.

### Earlier Low-Temperature Experiment

- Produced more smoke but leathery skin.
- Not currently preferred.
